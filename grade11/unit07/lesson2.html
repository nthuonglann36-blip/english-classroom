Pronunciation

Intonation in Wh- and Yes/No questions

10 minutes
Remember! · page 77

Rising ↗ or falling ↘?

Yes/No questions ↗

We usually use rising intonation: the voice goes up at the end of the sentence.

Example: Do you like it? ↗

Wh- questions ↘

We usually use falling intonation: the voice goes down at the end of the sentence.

Example: Where do you buy it? ↘

Grammar

Perfect gerunds and perfect participle clauses

15 minutes
Remember! · page 78 · perfect gerund = having + past participle · an action completed before the main verb

Perfect gerunds

  • 1️⃣

    Subject of a sentence

    Having studied science subjects made it easy for me to choose a university degree.

  • 2️⃣

    Object after admit, deny, forget, mention, regret, remember

    My friend didn’t remember having lent me his English textbook.

  • 3️⃣

    After prepositions

    My cousin often talked about having studied for five years at a top university.

Grammar · Task 1 · Find and correct the mistakes in the following sentences 5 min

Find and correct the mistakes

1. I forgot have discussed this topic with you. have → having discussed

2. Had won many maths competitions helped me win a place at university. Had → Having won

3. Nam regretted not having choose a more interesting course at university. choose → chosen

4. He was proud of had won the first place at the biology competition. had → having won

Remember! · page 78 · perfect participle clauses · same form: having asked, having studied

Perfect participle clauses

  • ⏮️

    An action before the main clause

    Having finished their course, they started looking for jobs.

  • 💡

    The reason for the main action

    Not having read the book, he can’t give us his opinion.

Gerund or participle clause? · same form, different job

Ask: is having won… the subject?

Perfect gerund = the subject

Having won the competition made Mai feel proud.

→ Having won… is the subject of made.

Perfect participle clause

Having won the competition, Mai felt proud.

→ a comma + its own subject (Mai): the earlier action.
