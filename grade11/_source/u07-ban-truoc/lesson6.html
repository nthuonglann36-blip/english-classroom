Vocabulary

3 key writing words

7 minutes
Vocabulary · LOOK → SAY → UNDERSTAND → USE

Three key words

  • enquiry /ɪnˈkwaɪəri/ (n) a question asking someone for information — câu hỏi, yêu cầu cung cấp thông tin
    In context: I am writing to make an enquiry about your cooking courses.
  • discount /ˈdɪskaʊnt/ (n) a reduction in the usual price — mức giảm giá, ưu đãi
    In context: Do you offer any discounts for low-income students?
  • apprenticeship /əˈprentɪʃɪp/ (n) a period of paid training while learning a trade — hình thức học nghề có lương
    In context: Could you tell me whether the school offers an apprenticeship programme?

Pre-writing

How to write indirect questions

10 minutes
Grammar focus · indirect questions — statement word order

Turn direct questions into polite enquiries

Rule: Use statement word order (subject + verb), not question order.

Do I need to take a test? → Could you tell me whether I need to take a test?

How much does it cost? → Could you please tell me how much the course costs?

When does it start? → Could you let me know when the course starts?

Notice: after whether (yes/no questions) and question word + statement (Wh- questions) — no auxiliary verb inversion. This previews/reinforces the grammar students will need in Task 1. Quick check: elicit one or two more examples from students.

Task 1 · complete the enquiries using the prompts

Complete the polite request sentences

  1. Could you please tell me ______________________? (Need to take a test?) if / whether I need to take a test
  2. I would also like to know ______________________. (Are there discounts for low-income students?) if / whether there are discounts for low-income students
  3. Could you let me know ______________________? (What is the course duration?) what the course duration is
Writing tools · a request letter has FIVE parts

Structure of a request letter

  1. 1 Greeting Dear Sir or Madam,
  2. 2 Purpose I am writing to enquire about…
  3. 3 Questions Could you please tell me…? / I would like to know…
  4. 4 Closing I look forward to hearing from you.
  5. 5 Sign-off Yours faithfully, / Yours sincerely, [Your name]

Polite language toolkit:

I am writing to ask for more information about… · I would like to enquire about…

Could you please tell me…? · I would also like to know… · Would it be possible to…?

Writing model · read AFTER drafting · highlight the purpose, enquiries and closing

Sample request letter

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to enquire about the tour guide training programme at SGV Vocational School. I am a school-leaver who is very interested in this field and would like more information before applying.

Firstly, could you please tell me whether I need to take an entrance test? Secondly, I would like to know how much the course fee is, and whether there are any discounts for low-income students. Finally, could you let me know when the next intake starts?

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours faithfully,

Nguyen Thi Mai

Peer review

Editor's checklist

8 minutes
Peer review · exchange letters · give TWO useful suggestions

Editor's checklist

  • Correct greeting (Dear Sir or Madam,) and closing (Yours faithfully,) Use Yours faithfully when you don't know the name; Yours sincerely when you do.
  • Clear purpose stated in the first sentence I am writing to enquire about… / I am writing to ask for information about…
  • At least THREE polite indirect questions Could you please tell me whether/what/when/how…? · I would like to know…
  • Formal tone throughout (no contractions, no slang) Not "I wanna know" → "I would like to know"; Not "Can you?" → "Could you please?"
  • Word count: 140–170 words Count and adjust. Too short? Add one more question. Too long? Remove one repetitive sentence.
Editing sprint · find the four mistakes · work in pairs

Improve these sentences

  1. Can you tell me how much does the fee cost? Could you tell me how much the fee costs? (indirect question → statement word order; Can → Could for politeness)
  2. I want infos about your courses. I would like information about your courses. (info is uncountable; want → would like for formal tone)
  3. Dear Mr/Ms, Dear Sir or Madam, (use when name is unknown)
  4. I look forward to hear from you. I look forward to hearing from you. (gerund after "look forward to")
Exit ticket · two minutes

What can you do now?

  1. One polite question stem I can use is… Could you please tell me…? · I would like to know… · Would it be possible to…?
  2. One thing I improved in my letter was… Encourage honest reflection — formal tone, correct grammar, appropriate structure.
