Vocabulary

4 words in context

8 minutes
Vocabulary · LOOK → SAY → UNDERSTAND → USE

Four key words

  • appointment /əˈpɔɪntmənt/ (n) an arranged meeting at a particular time — cuộc hẹn
    In context: I'd like to make an appointment to discuss education options.
  • reschedule /ˌriːˈʃedjuːl/ (v) to change the time or date of a planned event — đổi lịch hẹn
    In context: I'm afraid I have to reschedule — could we meet on Friday instead?
  • technical education /ˈteknɪkəl ˌedʒuˈkeɪʃən/ (n) training that develops practical skills for specific industries — giáo dục kỹ thuật
    In context: He chose technical education to become an electrician.
  • higher education /ˌhaɪər edʒuˈkeɪʃən/ (n) education at universities or similar institutions — giáo dục đại học
    In context: She pursued higher education and got a bachelor's degree in economics.

Everyday English

Making an appointment

15 minutes
Everyday English · useful expressions · follow the three stages

Making an appointment — three stages

Ask / Suggest a time

Would Friday afternoon suit you?

Will you be available on Monday?

Could I meet you on Thursday afternoon?

Decline / Reschedule

Sorry, I have another appointment then.

I'm afraid I'm not free on that day.

Could we make it Wednesday instead?

Confirm

Wednesday at 2 pm suits me fine. / That works for me.

So shall I come at 10 am? / See you then!

Culture

UK education after secondary school

12 minutes
Culture discovery · read the textbook overview · follow the possible routes

What happens after school in the UK?

Age 16 — School-leaving point

In England, learners must stay in education or training until 18.

They can choose: A-levels, vocational courses, apprenticeships.

Age 18 — Into the next stage

Options: technical education (BTEC, T-levels) or higher education (university).

University courses lead to a bachelor's degree.

Culture · Task 1 · complete the table — no more than TWO words or a number

  2. BTEC and T-levels are examples of ________. technical education
  3. Studying at a university is an example of ________. higher education

Culture comparison · use the prompts: Both… / However… / While… / In contrast…

After school: Viet Nam ↔ UK

🇻🇳 Viet Nam

Students may continue upper-secondary school until Grade 12 (age ~18).

Then: university entrance exam (THPTQG) or vocational college.

University: 4–5 years → bachelor's degree.

🇬🇧 UK (England)

Must stay in education/training until 18.

Routes: A-levels, BTECs, T-levels, apprenticeships.

University: 3 years → bachelor's degree.

Talk about TWO differences between the two systems. Both countries have vocational and academic pathways. However, in the UK, students must stay in education until 18 by law. In contrast, in Viet Nam, students choose to continue after Grade 9 or Grade 12.

Culture quiz · True or False? UK pathways · answer using the reading

True or False?

  • In England, learners must stay in education or training until 18. TRUE — England requires education/training until age 18.
  • Vocational education in the UK can only prepare learners for manual jobs. FALSE — Vocational education can prepare for a wide range of jobs and can even lead to higher education (e.g. via T-levels).
  • Students in the UK can do an apprenticeship instead of going to university. TRUE — Apprenticeships are one of the recognised pathways after age 16.
